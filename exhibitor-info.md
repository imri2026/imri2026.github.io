# Exhibitor Badges

The sponsor packages come with complimentary exhibitor badges. Additional exhibitor badges can be provided for additional cost. Please note that the exhibitor badges are provided in compliance with the hospital’s guidelines for industry participation and **differ from regular registration badges**. If a person from your company plans to present their scientific work at oral/poster sessions, this person must register using the regular registration page to meet the guideline. 

Here’s the excerpt from the guideline:
 
- An exhibitor is an industry representative from a company that engages in a fee-for-service transaction with the hospital for the opportunity to promote their goods and services at a Mass General Brigham Educational Activity.  Promotional opportunities must be  separate from, and not interfere with, the educational component of the educational activity and as such, the role of an exhibitor should be very limited. Please refer to the below guidelines for expectations regarding the role of exhibitors:
- Exhibitors must remain in areas specifically intended for exhibit space, separate from and set up so that they do not interfere with the educational component of the activity.
- Exhibitors are not to be provided free registration to a Mass General Brigham Educational Activity as a condition of their purchase of the promotional opportunity.
- Exhibitors may not attend any component of the educational activity, except for a didactic session or lecture if the following circumstances are present:
  - the conference sessions or lectures provide the opportunity to listen but not the opportunity to interact significantly with attendees;
  - there is room after all attendees are seated;
  - the exhibitor clearly discloses their role verbally to the audience prior to engaging in any discussion. The moderator of the session may reserve the right to limit participation by the industry representative if an industry representative’s contributions appear biased or promotional.
- Exhibitors may not attend meals, breaks, social and networking events, round table discussions, or any component of the educational activity where an exhibitor may engage in discussion that could be perceived as sales and/or marketing.  
- Exhibitors must wear a name badge clearly indicating their role as an exhibitor.
 

# Mass General Brigham (MGB) Guidelines

The 15th Interventional MRI Symposium is an Educational Activity organized by the Mass General Brigham (MGB), and the sponsor exhibition opportunities are provided in accordance with MGB Office for Interactions with Industry (OII)’s guideline on educational activities. Below are excerpts from relevant guidelines: 

## Guidelines for Industry Participation at Mass General Brigham Educational Activities
There are four scenarios in which industry representatives may be present at an MGB Educational Activity: as **a speaker or presenter**, as **a technical expert**, as **an exhibitor**, or as **an attendee/learner**. Guidelines and clarification on each scenario are presented below. Should a situation arise that does not meet one of these four scenarios, please consult with OII. 

### Speaker/Presenter

Industry representatives may attend an MGB Educational Activity as a speaker or presenter of a formal presentation or discussion as long as the following conditions are met: 
- The industry representative’s talk must be entirely oriented to science and medical education and may not involve promoting a product or their company; 
- The Program Director must select the speaker and confirm that the decision to include the industry representative is based on the medical/scientific/educational merit of the content they are presenting; 
- The industry representative must present a fair and balanced approach to the content; 
- If an industry representative’s company has provided commercial support to the educational activity, the opportunity to be a speaker must not be provided in exchange for the commercial support; and 
- The Program Director and/or Co-Chairs of the MGB Education Review Board must review and approve the industry representative’s slides. 

If the industry representative is an employee of the company, the following conditions also apply: 
- The content of their presentation must not be related to the business lines or products of the company they represent; or 
- The content of the activity must be limited to basic science research (e.g., pre-clinical research, drug discovery) or the processes/methodologies of research, themselves unrelated to a specific disease or compound/drug. The content of the presentation or discussion must not be related to clinical applications of the research/discovery or clinical recommendations concerning the business lines or products of their employer; or 
- The representative must be presenting as a technician teaching the safe and proper use of medical devices. 
  
### Technical Expert

An industry representative may be present at an MGB Educational Activity to provide technical support or answer specific technical questions regarding the company’s equipment or to support an in-kind grant. Any presentation about the company’s equipment must be led by MGB faculty. 

### Exhibitor

An industry representative may be present at an MGB Educational Activity as an exhibitor. For specific guidelines regarding exhibits, please refer to the Guidelines for Promotional Opportunities in Conjunction with MGB Educational Activities. Please note that it is not permissible for exhibits to be present in a hospital facility. 

An exhibitor is an industry representative from a company that engages in a fee-for-service transaction with the hospital for the opportunity to promote their goods and services at an MGB Educational Activity.  Promotional opportunities must be separate from, and not interfere with, the educational component of the educational activity and as such, the role of an exhibitor should be very limited. Please refer to the below guidelines for expectations regarding the role of exhibitors: 

- Exhibitors must remain in areas specifically intended for exhibit space, separate from and set up so that they do not interfere with the educational component of the activity; 
- Exhibitors are not to be provided free registration to an MGB Educational Activity as a condition of their purchase of the promotional opportunity; 
- Exhibitors may not attend any component of the educational activity, except for a didactic session or lecture if the following circumstances are present: 
  - the conference sessions or lectures provide the opportunity to listen but not the opportunity to interact significantly with attendees; 
  - there is room after all attendees are seated; 
  - the exhibitor clearly discloses their role verbally to the audience prior to engaging in any discussion. The moderator of the session may reserve the right to limit participation by the industry representative if an industry representative’s contributions appear biased or promotional. 
- Exhibitors may not attend meals, breaks, social and networking events, round table discussions, or any component of the educational activity where an exhibitor may engage in discussion that could be perceived as sales and/or marketing; 
- Exhibitors must wear a name badge clearly indicating their role as an exhibitor. 
  
  
###  Attendee/Learner

Industry physicians may attend MGB Educational Activities, not on behalf of the company, but as learners only. They must meet all registration requirements and register and pay tuition like all other attendees.
 
## Guidelines for Promotional Opportunities in Conjunction with Mass General Brigham Educational Activities – Exhibit B: Specific Guidelines for Promotional Opportunities

### Advertisement and Use of Company Logo

**In General**
- A company logo may not be visible in areas where the learning is taking place or anywhere that is in the direct/obligate path of the attendees’ learning experience. Accordingly, posters, banners, etc. may not be placed in any area that is not designated for exhibit space;
- A promotional opportunity may not be offered that involves placing a company’s name and/or logo on room key cards, lanyards, napkins, tote bags, or any other items related to the conference. 

**In Program Materials**
- Program brochures that contain educational content may not contain promotional opportunities, including advertising, corporate logos, trade names or a product-group message. Such brochures may only list the companies that have provided commercial support for the program with a disclosure stating, “Hospital has received support from the following companies in the form of educational grants: (list companies).” 
- Program materials that do not contain any educational content of the program (such as a program booklet containing an agenda, course logistics, etc.) may include (i) company advertisements; and (ii) the name and logo of any company that has purchased promotional opportunities at the program (e.g., exhibits).  However, company advertisements must be placed in the back of the materials, separated by a blank page and titled “Advertisements”.  

**On a Program Website** (a) The names and logos of companies that have purchased promotional opportunities may be displayed on the MGB Activity’s website, provided they are posted on a separate tab, so that viewers can peruse the information about the educational activity without coming into contact with the company promotional material, if they so choose. When posting exhibitors’ company names/logos on the educational activity’s website under a separate tab, the program should assure that a  click on the company’s logo, name or other link that takes viewers to the exhibit sponsor’s/exhibitor’s website is accompanied by a dialogue pop-up box that appears on the computer screen informing viewers that they are leaving the MGB site and about to enter a third-party site, along with a prompt asking viewers if they wish to continue.  (b) When sending emails about the educational activity to potential attendees, a department may include a link to the exhibit sponsor/exhibit tab of the educational activity’s website at the bottom of the email, but it may not list the names/logos of promotional opportunity sponsors in the email. 


### Exhibit Space

It is not permissible to sell exhibit tables for an MGB Educational Activity that will take place in a hospital facility. Other promotional opportunities for MGB Educational Activities in a hospital facility must be reviewed on a case by case basis. 

For an MGB Educational Activity that will take place in a location other than in a hospital facility, the sale of exhibit space is generally permissible as long as the exhibit space is sufficiently separated from the attendees’ learning experience. Additional guidelines to consider:
- Exclusive and/or non-exclusive formal company lectures may not be held in the exhibit hall;
- Meals or snacks for the educational activity may not be placed in the exhibit hall; 
- Exhibitors must wear a name badge clearly indicating their role as an exhibitor; 
- Appropriate disclaimer language must be posted wherever exhibitors are listed, including at the entrance of the exhibit hall, on the program website, and in a program brochure that informs attendees that the exhibitors have no control over the activity’s content and that neither the program nor the hospital endorses the exhibitors or its products; 
- Exhibitors may not distribute any type of branded or unbranded products or items (e.g. food, pens, notepads), hold a raffle or giveaways; 
- Exhibitors may not get advance access to attendee registration lists or attendee demographics; and
- Free registration for the educational activity may not be provided as a benefit to exhibitors. 

For MGB Educational Activities intended for patients:
- A disclaimer must be posted at the entrance to the exhibit hall clearly indicating that the MGB hospital or other MGB entity neither supports nor endorses the information or products advertised by the companies, and that patients should discuss any information that they learn while visiting the exhibit booths with their physician and/or other healthcare providers; 
- Companies may distribute the following to patients: 
  - Informational materials about the company and its products; 
  - Educational materials about the disease state and treatment options discussed at the educational activity; 
  - Unbranded items related to the disease state that is the subject of the program that are of modest value; 
- Companies may NOT distribute the following:
  - Branded products or items; 
  - Unbranded products (e.g. pens, notepads) that are not specifically designed for the disease state being discussed at the educational activity; 
  - Candies or other items that are not related to the specific disease being discussed at the educational activity.
